# Hive art spec

The reference sets the quality bar; these lines set the look.

1. The 3D scene is the page: it fills the viewport edge to edge, with text laid over it, not boxed beside it.
2. The scene is a detailed, legible hive with depth, light and shadow: comb walls of many hexagonal cells, and a viewer can tell chambers, cells and bees apart.
3. One warm, restrained palette: honey amber and wax gold on deep brown, with dark empty cells for gaps and pale grey sealed cells for absent parts; no flat primary-colour tiles.
4. The world feels alive: bees move along paths between chambers, and light or camera drift slowly, without clutter.
5. Typography is deliberate: monospace small-caps labels and a clear hierarchy; no wall of raw paths or IDs.
6. Text sits in one frosted card or rail over the scene, short and readable, not stacked cards of data dumps.
7. At 380 px the page still reads as the same designed object, not a long column of cards.
