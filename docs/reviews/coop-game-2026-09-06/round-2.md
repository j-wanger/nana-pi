### A. Totality + determininism (§3.1–3.7)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** Liquid occupancy, buoyancy, and fragile breaking still do not define a total transition. Fluids are entities and one occupant is allowed per true cell, while collision forbids entering a cell containing any entity; therefore a player or box can never be “in liquid,” so `density` cannot trigger. Even if co-occupancy were intended, “rises to the surface” has no direction when gravity is off or its axis is collapsed. Separately, when a falling box encounters a fragile solid, “breaks when an entity falls onto it” permits at least three outcomes: the box remains above the now-broken cell, enters that cell, or continues through it in the same tick. The resulting per-cell mutation and collision ordering are unspecified. Player pushing is not defined either: under §3.3 a box simply makes the destination non-enterable, so box-on-plate puzzles currently must rely exclusively on gravity or buoyancy.

### B. Solver + generator (§3.8, §6)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The canonical tuple omits mutable room-cell state, so two worlds that differ only because a fragile voxel has broken serialize identically despite having different successors; it also does not name immutable entity kind/material data needed to interpret density, although that could be puzzle-static metadata. More fundamentally, the solver cannot yet construct its action graph because §9 leaves reachable facings and perspective inputs open, while gravity changes are referenced by ablation without defining who may perform which changes. Ablation is also not an operation yet: “forbidding that mode, or that gravity change” does not say whether to remove an entire mode family, one facing, one transition, or one target gravity setting, and “decision depth” has no computable definition. Exit and box-on-plate are enough predicates for ten puzzles in principle, but action grammar, complete dynamic state, and an exact ablation transform must exist before generation; adding them later would invalidate solver proofs and canonical hashes.

### C. Fun-risk

**FINDING (severity: major):** The highest unreadability risk is hidden-depth collision. Viewed from +X, place passable water at `x=3` and a solid at the player’s frozen `x=0` destination: the projected square visibly presents water as front-most and passable, but the attempted move silently fails because of the unseen solid at `x=0`. That behavior is deterministic yet indistinguishable from ignored input in the smallest playable. The smallest fix is to require reduced-mode rendering to display the mover-specific enterability result—such as a blocked overlay or ghost of the true-depth obstruction—on adjacent destinations, preserving frozen-depth collision without hiding its cause.

### D. Fold check

**FINDING (severity: major):** V2 faithfully folds A1–A3, B1–B3, D1–D3, E1/E2, F1, and F3, and mostly folds A4, but it only partially folds C1–C3. C1 required every mutable field in canonical state, yet broken fragile cells are absent; C2’s causal-ablation principle appears, but the intervention and decision-depth metric remain undefined; and C3’s deferred generator contract—stable starts, explicit legal actions, dead-state handling, symmetry normalization rather than an ordinary hash, and a declared omniscient solver policy—is not recorded. A4’s identity invariant is also softened into a vacuous condition: mode switching exists only as input to `step`, and every such step reaches physics stage 4, so the public transition API provides no way to switch and reverse “with no tick.”

VERDICT: BLOCK — The liquid and fragile rules still admit no unique transition, and the solver lacks both complete state and a defined action/ablation model.
