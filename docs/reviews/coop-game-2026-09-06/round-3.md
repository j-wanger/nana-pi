### A. Fold check

**FINDING (severity: major):** Liquids-as-cells, the single fragile outcome, mutable-grid state, static entity metadata, hidden-depth overlays, terminal losses, and no-time switches faithfully fold the corresponding round-2 findings. The pushing and action/ablation folds remain incomplete: in collapsed-X view, put the player at `(0,1,0)` and a visibly adjacent box at `(2,1,1)`; moving `+Z` enters the player’s empty true destination `(0,1,1)` but the projected destination is occupied by the box, so “move into a box’s cell” does not determine whether the player moves, is blocked, or remotely pushes the box at frozen `X=2`. Likewise, removing a view only “from the action set” does not remove its capabilities when it is the start view. Nothing from C or D was dropped, but B’s exact action graph and ablation transform were softened rather than completed.

### B. Totality + determinism

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** Several new worlds still lack a unique successor. Push a sinking box horizontally into a liquid cell with two empty cells below under gravity `−Y`: §3.1 can mean liquid motion replaces gravity and moves it one cell, while §3.7’s separate “gravity step …; buoyancy” can move it once for gravity and again for sinking. Put a floating player below a sinking box with one empty liquid cell between them: both target that cell, but §3.7 states bottom-up ordering for “gravity” and then separately names buoyancy, so it does not establish whether that ordering governs both opposing liquid motions; a floating entity directly below a box similarly may remain blocked or follow after the box moves. A push toward a fragile solid is at least determinately blocked—it does not break because only gravity breaks fragile cells—but the reduced-depth push above remains undefined. Re-embedding is ordered, including destruction mid-stack, but switching to 3D supplies no “collapsed axis” along which repair or destruction can occur. Finally, side view always claims two moves even when gravity is collapsed and both visible axes are non-gravity axes, while dry entities can never increase their coordinate against gravity; gravity-on rooms can still use horizontal movement, pushing, falling, and switching, but no-jump makes every dry upward exit categorically unreachable rather than merely difficult.

### C. Solver

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** BFS is finite and terminating over the bounded tuple, but it is not exact until the transitions above are total. The ablation is also not computable as intended when the removed view is the initial view: a puzzle starting in side `+X` can continue moving there after only the `switch(+X)` target is removed, falsely treating that view as ablated. “Number of switches in the shortest solution” is not single-valued when equal-length shortest paths contain different switch counts; it must specify a lexicographic objective or minimum/maximum over shortest paths. Symmetry normalization is unsound as written because gravity and the goal are transformed but the current view/camera facing and action directions are not: reflecting a side-`+X` state across X must produce side-`−X`, or hashing the two together can merge states with different projected collisions and successors.

### D. Smallest playable

**FINDING (severity: major):** Build the reduced-depth push room first: gravity `−Y`, a common solid floor, player at `(0,1,0)`, box at `(2,1,1)`, collapsed-X side view from `+X`, and an exit beyond the box’s projected square. The decisive input is one `+Z` move. The rule is falsified if independent implementations—or the renderer’s legality preview and the headless solver—disagree among moving the player to `(0,1,1)`, blocking the move, and pushing the box to `(2,1,2)`; v3 currently licenses all three readings and therefore cannot supply the solver’s expected successor.

VERDICT: BLOCK — Reduced-mode pushing and liquid-step ordering still prevent a total transition function, while ablation and symmetry normalization remain unsound.
