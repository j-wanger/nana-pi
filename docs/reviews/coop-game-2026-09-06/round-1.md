## A. Projection + re-embedding rules (§3.3, §3.5, §3.6)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** `PUSH OUT` is not a total rule. In a three-cell X-axis room viewed from +X, put the player at x=0 and solids at x=1 and x=2; while reduced, an allowed material edit changes the material under x=0 from gas/none to solid. On expansion the player is inside solid, but there is no cell toward the camera into which it can be pushed. The rules do not say whether to push through walls, search away from the camera, crush/kill the entity, reject the prior edit, or declare the state invalid. They also do not define whether “out” requires an empty cell or merely a non-solid one already occupied by fluid or another entity.

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** Projected collision can both reject legal true-space movement and permit true-cell overlap. Viewed from +X, a solid at x=3 makes a projected cell impassable even when the moving entity’s frozen x=0 cell is empty. Conversely, put passable water at x=3 and a hidden solid—or entity B—at x=0. The water is front-most, so entity A frozen at x=0 can enter under projected collision, ending inside the hidden solid or in B’s exact true cell. Re-embedding only handles “inside solid,” not entity/entity overlap. The rules need a complete occupancy model, collision layers, whether entities participate in FRONT-MOST, and a deterministic conflict-resolution rule covering multiple entities and failed placement.

**FINDING (severity: major):** Nested 1D projection is underdefined and non-commutative. After collapsing X from +X, place A at `(x=2,y=0,z=0)` and B at `(x=3,y=1,z=0)`. Collapsing Y from +Y next selects B because Y is the outer/front-most comparison; reversing the collapse order selects according to X first and can select A. “Fixed order” does not name that order, the second camera side, the retained line, or behavior when 1D is entered from top rather than side view; §9 explicitly leaves the player’s row open. The state must encode the ordered projection stack and camera sign at each level, with a lexicographic visibility rule.

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The no-move round trip is asserted but not implied. Put a box unsupported above a floor, switch from side view to top view so gravity disappears, then immediately switch back; §3.6 says physics runs after re-embedding, so the box can fall even though no player movement occurred. Likewise, a material edit or autonomous fluid/fire tick between switches changes the result. “Re-embedding is idempotent” also conflicts with including physics: applying it twice can produce two falling steps. The design must separate pure embedding repair from physics, define exact tick boundaries, and either narrow identity to “no elapsed ticks, edits, autonomous transitions, or inputs” or explicitly snapshot/restore a no-op round trip.

## B. Physics in visible dimensions (§3.4)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** Top-view fluids contradict frozen coordinates. Suppose the pool surface is at y=1 and the player approaches at y=3. FRONT-MOST shows the pool surface and `passableFromTop` permits entry, but §3.5 freezes the player at y=3, so the player walks over the pool while hovering two cells above it; approaching from y=0 instead leaves the player under or inside the same projected surface. The rules need to choose between frozen depth and surface snapping, and must define displacement, drowning, occupancy, and restoration when leaving top view.

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** A mid-fall mode flip has no defined state transition. If a box has downward velocity when Y is collapsed, “drop the component” could mean erase velocity, suspend it for restoration, or discard only acceleration while preserving momentum; these produce respectively a stopped box, resumed fall, or hidden-axis motion. Bounciness and friction likewise require velocity or an explicitly quasi-static cellular replacement, yet velocity is absent from the solver state bound. Tick order for player input, projection, collision, mode change, and autonomous physics must be specified.

**FINDING (severity: major):** Gravity into the screen can create arbitrary suspension rather than merely an interesting lever. A falling box can be frozen unsupported by entering a side view whose collapsed axis matches gravity, moved laterally through geometry selected at another depth, then released by expanding. That may be acceptable, but the design has no rule for hidden contacts, fluid pressure, or whether gravity-off states must remain re-embeddable. Six directions plus on/off is enough only for memoryless one-cell gravity; it is not enough for the promised friction, bouncing, liquids, and mode flips without velocity, update-order, and settling rules.

## C. Solver + generator (§3.7, §6)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The stated state-space product is incomplete and does not establish finiteness. It omits remaining edit budget, mutable material-property values, mode facing, the ordered 1D projection stack, velocities or phase, pending deaths/resolution, and agent preview/commit state. Once elements exist, fire/water/frozen/electrified state is generally per voxel, making the bound exponential in room volume; numeric tool arguments can make the action space infinite unless every property has a finite enumerated domain. The design needs an explicit canonical state tuple, finite domains, transition function, action grammar, and hash/equality rule.

**FINDING (severity: major):** The non-triviality tests are gameable and sometimes too strict. A long empty corridor satisfies minimum solution length by padding movement, while one perspective switch that disables gravity can be counted as touching both “perspective” and “gravity” even when there is only one obvious action. Conversely, an elegant puzzle built entirely around repeated perspective occlusion fails the ≥2-systems rule. Require causal necessity—such as shortest-solution ablation showing that each counted system or edit is indispensable—rather than event counting, and measure decision depth rather than raw input length.

**FINDING (severity: major):** The generator lacks the constraints needed to produce valid cooperative puzzles: stable starts, total re-embedding, reachable resets, dead-state detection, legal goal predicates, symmetry/duplicate rejection, bounded edit domains, all-solutions non-triviality, and difficulty metrics. It must also say whether it solves a perfect-information joint policy, a human partial-observation policy assisted by inspections, or merely one omniscient action sequence. “The agent judges the hint” needs a verified hint contract so it cannot reveal an unverified shortcut or frame a puzzle whose claimed mechanic is not actually required.

## D. The agent boundary (§5)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The preview/commit split does not provide the claimed human confirmation. In the kit, `mutates:true` only causes a clicked block action to compose a prompt without sending it; listing `commit_edit` as manifest-mutating merely triggers `agent:changed` after execution. The model can call `preview_edit` and then `commit_edit` in the same turn without a gate. Commit must require an actual host confirmation or a one-shot opaque capability bound to the preview payload, puzzle, world revision, cost, session, expiry, and user approval; stale or replayed capabilities must fail.

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** Zod validates shape, not authority. Unless semantic checks expressly forbid it, tool arguments could target the budget, goal, gravity rules, world dimensions, arbitrary entity fields, or a globally shared material whose mutation has unpriced multiplicative effects. Commit could also submit different arguments from preview or race intervening player movement. The contract needs strict schemas plus immutable-field and target allowlists, bounded property domains, server-computed cost, revision checks, atomic commit, and validation of the resulting world against engine invariants.

**FINDING (severity: major):** The replay and honesty audit claim is overstated. The block ledger can prove which signed inspections and previews were returned, but it does not by itself record every player input, perspective switch, physics tick, random seed, world revision, rejected edit, or exact committed transition needed to replay the game. It also cannot prove that free-form “agent’s reading” accurately or completely reports hidden truth; it only exposes evidence for later comparison. The claim should be narrowed to tool provenance unless the game adds a canonical transition log and a checker linking narrative claims to inspected facts.

## E. The menu test (§2, §10.2)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The v1 interaction table is menu-sized: **mode** = 3D, four side facings, top; **gravity** = ±X/±Y/±Z × on/off, with a component either active or erased by projection; **movement** = visible-axis step with frozen hidden coordinates, followed by push/fall on expansion; **material edit** = choose target × property × allowed value. In the smallest slice, `flammability`, `conductivity`, `opacity`, and `reflectivity` do nothing; hardness has no breaking rule; density has no defined buoyancy rule; friction and bounciness only modify underspecified gravity motion; state/passability drive collision. A target/property/value dialog plus an inspect-depth button can replace the agent exactly.

**FINDING (severity: major):** Adding ordinary fire/water interactions alone increases world combinations but does not make the agent’s edit language open—the validated action remains an enumerable property form. The minimum credible addition is a bounded compositional rule-edit grammar, such as predicates over contact/material/state/mode joined to engine-defined effects, with solver validation and budgeted complexity. Elements should activate that grammar, but merely exposing more property rows still produces a larger menu rather than an agent-forcing interface.

## F. Kit fit + subtraction (§7, §8, §4)

**FINDING (severity: blocker):** The live game has no state-sharing path in the kit. Human movement and perspective changes occur in the browser, while `inspect` and `commit_edit` execute in the separate pi child; the existing app listener serves pages and fixed-argv GET data, but provides no browser-to-authoritative-sim mutation channel. A browser-only world is invisible to tools, while a child-owned world cannot receive direct player inputs. This requires a game server/shared durable state plus authenticated or same-origin action and event routes—substantially more than a block type—or a bespoke listener.

**FINDING (severity: major):** The slice already declares another forbidden kit change: converting the shared drawer into a collapsible floating bubble requires `stage.js`/`stage.css`, while the transferability rule says anything beyond a block type fails. Heavy runtime synchronization, exact revision refresh, and hidden-information presentation are also not supplied by `agent:changed`, which is only a generic completion notification and even fires with an error flag. Either rule the game bespoke now or explicitly amend and re-review the kit boundary.

**FINDING (severity: major):** V1 should subtract inert future properties and 1D. Flammability, conductivity, opacity, and reflectivity have no behavior before elements/light; hardness and density also lack operative rules, and 1D’s retained line is unresolved and absent from the smallest playable. These can be added through a versioned property registry later. What cannot safely wait is the canonical authoritative state, transition/tick order, finite edit domains, revisioned event log, reset/death semantics, and browser/tool synchronization, because the solver, replay, and every generated puzzle depend on them.

VERDICT: BLOCK — The ratified core is not a total deterministic transition system, the v1 agent surface is replaceable by a menu, and the game requires a state-sharing kit mechanism that the current architecture does not provide.
