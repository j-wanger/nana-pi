## How this file is organized

The rows are grouped into a tree: capability → feature → subgroup. A capability heading reads
`# CAP-nn · <label>`, a feature heading `## FEAT-nnn · <label>` and a subgroup heading
`### SUB-nnn · <label>`. Each heading is followed by a `Purpose:` line saying why that part of the
product exists; a capability also carries an `Outcome:` line, and a feature a `Status:` line.
Every row lives in exactly one table — its home — under one feature, or under one subgroup when
its feature is subdivided. A feature that also depends on requirements homed elsewhere names them
on its `Also applies:` line; a requirement named there is shared, and the outline flags it as high
blast radius. R-287 to R-295 check this structure. `docs/requirements-tree.md` is the outline of
the tree (every heading's ID, label, purpose, status, parent, row count, row IDs and shared
requirements), generated by `npm run req:tree`; the suite fails when it is stale, so any change to
the tree shows there as a compact diff. Where the old numbered sections went is in
`docs/requirements-sections-2026-10-09.md`.

## How the tree is governed

Who decides. Jake decides every change to the tree's shape: creating, renaming, moving, splitting,
merging, freezing or retiring a capability, feature or subgroup, and moving a row to another home.
The seat proposes such changes; a change brief declares them under "Tree changes", and the review
reads them in the outline diff. A builder adds or changes rows only in the feature its brief names.

A feature's life. `Status:` reads one of: `proposed` (agreed, no rows yet), `active`,
`frozen (<ruling and when it ends>)` — no new work on it until the ruling changes — or
`retired (<successor or reason>)`. A retired feature keeps its heading and ID and holds no rows.
Splitting or merging features creates new IDs; the old feature is retired naming its successors.
Capability, feature and subgroup IDs are never renumbered or reused.

A requirement's life. A new behaviour starts as a requirement diff in its home feature, then
tagged tests, then code. Statuses and the ID rule are as stated above. A requirement listed on any
feature's `Also applies:` line is shared: a change to it names its home and every feature that
lists it, and the review reads them all. Retiring a requirement removes it from every
`Also applies:` line in the same change. Splitting or merging requirements creates new IDs; the
old row is retired with "superseded by" in its Evidence.

## Purpose and outcomes

Purpose: a customized agentic coding experience that can be used for any development project,
and makes development more controlled, robust and effective (Jake, 2026-10-09). It serves the
objective in `~/nana-agent-loop/OBJECTIVE.md`: build products with agents, with nana-pi as the
shared toolkit and experience.

Outcomes (astra's 2026-10-09 draft, agreed by Jake):

1. **Understand what the product enables.** Find a capability, understand why it matters, and reach its relevant obligations without reading the whole ledger.
2. **Delegate one coherent change.** Distinguish the agreed increment from adjacent discoveries, blockers and future work.
3. **Accept a change with useful evidence.** See the behavior changed, the requirement delta, the candidate revision, the checks performed and the remaining uncertainty.
4. **Reuse the experience across projects.** Adopt the appropriate workflow and controls without an unrelated toolchain migration.
5. **Continue without reconstructing history.** Resume the current goal, scope, decisions and evidence with the right context available.
